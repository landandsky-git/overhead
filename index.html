Overhead

Point your phone at the night sky to see which satellites are there. Tracks about 16,000 satellites, including Starlink. Gold dots are lit by the sun, and the bigger the dot, the brighter the satellite looks.

Needs location and motion access. Works best outdoors, away from metal and magnets, after waving the phone in a figure-8 to settle the compass.

Show on the sky

Sunlit satellites are drawn bigger and brighter the brighter they should look, on the same scale as the stars. Each satellite's typical brightness comes from measurements where they exist (Mike McCants' catalog, published Starlink studies), otherwise from its radar size or type. The app then adjusts for distance and angle to the sun. Expect it to be within about one magnitude.

Compass correction: 0°

Phone compasses point to magnetic north and are often a few degrees off. The easiest fix is the ◎ button: line up a bright star, planet or the Moon and the app sets this for you. You can also nudge it by hand.

Help

Why can't I see a satellite the app shows?

It may be in Earth's shadow (a blue dot), too faint for your sky, washed out by twilight or moonlight, or behind clouds. Satellites also look brightest with the Sun behind you. If everything seems shifted, line up the compass with ◎.

What does magnitude mean?

Astronomers' brightness scale, where lower numbers are brighter. Venus is about −4, the brightest stars around 0, and magnitude 6 is the faintest you can see in a very dark sky.

How accurate is it?

Positions are usually within a fraction of a degree, because the satellite data updates every few hours. The phone's compass is the biggest error, which aligning with ◎ fixes. Brightness is an estimate, usually within about one magnitude.

Does it work without signal?

Yes. Once you've opened it with signal, the app and its satellite data are saved on your phone. Data older than a week gets less accurate.

Why does the view jump when I point straight up?

Your phone may be switching between portrait and landscape. Turn on rotation lock while you use the app.
